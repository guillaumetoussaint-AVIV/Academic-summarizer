# Regional Science — Domain Skill

## Scope

This skill covers **spatial analysis and regional dynamics** as they relate to residential real estate. Regional science sits at the intersection of economics, geography, and planning, with a distinctive emphasis on **formal spatial modeling** and **multi-scale analysis**.

## Core Theoretical Frameworks

### New Economic Geography (NEG)
- **Krugman (1991) core-periphery model**: increasing returns, transport costs, and factor mobility generating spatial agglomeration.
- **Takahashi (2022) aging and agglomeration effects**: aging, spatial concentration, mobility.
- **Agglomeration economies**: localization (Marshall-Arrow-Romer), urbanization (Jacobs), matching, sharing, learning mechanisms (Duranton & Puga, 2004).
- **Centripetal vs. centrifugal forces**: congestion, land prices, and commuting costs as dispersing forces; knowledge spillovers and market access as concentrating forces.
- **Residential implications**: how production agglomeration drives housing demand geography; amenity-led vs. productivity-led growth.

### Spatial Equilibrium
- **Rosen-Roback framework**: inter-city equilibrium where real wages (adjusted for housing costs and amenities) equalize across locations for marginal movers.
- **Moretti (2011) spatial equilibrium**: nominal vs. real wage gaps; cost-of-living adjustments; implications for inequality measurement.
- **Diamond (2016)**: spatial sorting by education and the feedback loop between college-educated concentration and urban amenity provision.
- **Spatial disequilibrium**: when adjustment is slow — housing supply constraints, migration frictions, institutional rigidities.

### Migration and Mobility
- **Gravity models**: migration flows as a function of origin/destination size and distance (Zipf, Tinbergen); augmented gravity with push-pull factors.
- **Residential mobility**: intra-urban moves; life-cycle triggers (household formation, retirement); tenure transitions.
- **Sorting and selection**: who moves, who stays; self-selection bias in migration studies (Borjas, 1987; Dahl, 2002).
- **Spatial labor market adjustment**: Blanchard & Katz (1992) VAR framework; speed of adjustment across US states/regions.

### Spatial Structure and Polycentricity
- **Monocentricity vs. polycentricity**: measuring urban spatial structure; employment sub-centers identification (McMillen, 2001; Redfearn, 2007).
- **Edge cities and suburban employment**: decentralization of employment; implications for commuting patterns and residential price gradients.
- **Urban scaling laws**: city size distributions (Zipf's law, Gibrat's law); rank-size regularities; deviations and their explanations.
- **Functional urban areas**: defining metropolitan boundaries; commuting zones; morphological vs. functional definitions (OECD, Eurostat).

### Regional Growth and Convergence
- **Beta-convergence and sigma-convergence**: do lagging regions catch up? Unconditional vs. conditional convergence.
- **Club convergence**: multiple steady states; persistence of spatial inequality; Quah (1996) distribution dynamics.
- **Place-based vs. people-based policies**: Kline & Moretti (2014); spatially targeted subsidies; Enterprise Zones; Opportunity Zones.
- **Housing and regional adjustment**: housing supply as a mediating factor in regional growth (Hsieh & Moretti, 2019 — misallocation from housing constraints).

### Spatial Interaction and Accessibility
- **Accessibility measures**: Hansen (1959) gravity-based; cumulative opportunity; utility-based (logsum).
- **Transport and housing**: transit capitalization; highway effects on suburbanization; TOD (Transit-Oriented Development).
- **Commuting**: wasteful commuting debate (Hamilton, 1982; Small & Song, 1992); excess commuting; co-location of housing and jobs.

## Vocabulary Guide

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spatial autocorrelation | Statistical dependence between values at nearby locations (positive: similar values cluster; negative: dissimilar values alternate) |
| Spatial heterogeneity | Variation in relationships or processes across space (non-stationarity) |
| MAUP (Modifiable Areal Unit Problem) | Sensitivity of statistical results to the spatial unit of aggregation |
| Ecological fallacy | Inferring individual-level relationships from aggregate (area-level) data |
| Spatial lag | Value of a variable at neighboring locations, typically constructed via a spatial weights matrix (W) |
| Spatial weights matrix (W) | Formal representation of spatial relationships among observations (contiguity, distance, k-nearest neighbors) |
| Moran's I | Global measure of spatial autocorrelation |
| LISA (Local Indicators of Spatial Association) | Local measures of clustering (hot spots, cold spots, spatial outliers) |
| Agglomeration shadow | Area around a large city where smaller cities are suppressed due to competition |
| Market potential | Gravity-weighted sum of economic mass accessible from a location |
| Functional Urban Area (FUA) | City plus its commuting zone; the economically integrated urban region |
| Spatial spillover | Effect of a variable in one location on outcomes in neighboring locations |

## What to Emphasize in Summaries

1. **Scale of analysis**: What spatial unit is used (tract, ZIP, municipality, MSA, region, country)? Is the MAUP addressed?
2. **Spatial dependence**: Is spatial autocorrelation tested and modeled? What spatial weights structure is used?
3. **Equilibrium vs. dynamics**: Is the paper analyzing a cross-sectional equilibrium or a dynamic adjustment process?
4. **Spatial interaction**: Are flows (migration, commuting, trade, investment) modeled? What distance decay is assumed?
5. **Policy evaluation**: If place-based policies are studied, what is the counterfactual? Are displacement/spillover effects considered?
6. **Multi-scale perspective**: Does the paper acknowledge that processes at one scale (neighborhood) may differ from another (region)?
